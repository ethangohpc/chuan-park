## The two structural arguments

**Low 5-room supply.** HDB's build mix has run to 4-room and smaller since the
mid-2010s — smaller households, tighter land, entry-price affordability. In the
central region, 5-room flats were 642 of 14,387 units launched since 2015 (4.5%);
4-room over 64%. Punggol Northshore, launched 2015–16 for young families, follows the
same logic. 406B holds 22 five-room units against a 4-room pool in the several
hundreds across the three clusters. The tranche cannot be replenished locally: the
Edge/Cove/Vista 5-rooms are locked until ~2031.

Pricing consequence: the subject 5-room starts from $790,000 on 1,206 sq ft = $655 psf,
against the 4-room record of $870,000 at 421B (~$870 psf on a ~1,000 sq ft
floorplate ▲). ~25% psf discount for ~200 more sq ft, in the scarcer tranche.

**No true north–south orientation.** The Northshore grid was set by the coastline and
Northshore Drive, not the compass — blocks run parallel to the water to maximise sea
views and capture the north-east monsoon breeze. Every stack in Northshore Residences
I & II sits on the NW/SE axis; StraitsView rotates further, giving N/NE sea frontage
and S/SW road frontage. There is no N–S stack to wait for, so the decision is which
half of the NW/SE axis: SE takes morning sun and cools by afternoon; NW takes the full
late-afternoon load on the long facade. Under Period 9 feng shui (2024–2063) N and S
rank first with NW/SE immediately behind — second-best, not compromised.

## Site evidence — the level-4 view (corrects the earlier read)

A walk-through video filmed 11 Sep 2026 from the unit shows an open corridor between
two blocks looking directly onto the Straits of Johor — shipping lane, Johor coastline
and port cranes in frame, above the rooftop garden deck. This overturns the earlier
working assumption that level 4 in this estate sits below the view line.

Consequence for pricing: Northshore's market applies a reflex low-floor discount that
assumes no water view. This unit has one. The position is not "$100k under value" — it
is that the unit is priced as if level 4 sees nothing, and level 4 sees the Straits.

Residual risks: the corridor is framed, not open frontage (it is not what a 16th-floor
StraitsView stack sells), and maturing trees on the deck below could close the
sightline over time.
